## Keep a proposal-cake message short enough to read

The cake inscription is one part of the proposal, so make it understandable at a glance. A name plus a short question is usually easier to fit than a long quotation. Before sending the order, write the exact words in one line and check spelling, punctuation, and capitalisation. If you want the question hidden until the box opens, tell the bakery where it should appear and ask whether the chosen cake's surface can carry it clearly.

White Cakes hand-pipes buttercream designs. The 4-inch Bento has a small writing area and is best suited to a brief line; longer wording may crowd the design. A 6-inch custom cake gives a different amount of space, but the final layout depends on the finish. Send the message and a reference image early so the baker can confirm what will fit instead of guessing from a sample photo.

If the cake is part of a surprise, send the buyer's contact details for order questions and separately provide the delivery receiver's mobile number and full address. Confirm who should receive the cake, the delivery window, and any gate or lobby instructions. Keep the cake out of direct sun and hot cars while the moment is being arranged.

Ask for the date and availability at least three days ahead; the order is confirmed only after White Cakes replies with the agreed details and receives the 50% deposit. This short-message advice can live in the existing Cebu cake-message guide. A standalone proposal-message gallery needs original examples and clear confirmation that each line fits the cake surface.
