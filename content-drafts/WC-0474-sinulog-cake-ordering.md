## Avoid these preventable gaps in a Sinulog cake order

The first mistake is giving only the event name and leaving out the date. Sinulog plans can involve several days, so tell the bakery exactly when the cake is needed and ask whether that date is available. White Cakes asks for at least three days' notice; contacting the kitchen earlier gives more room to confirm a detailed hand-piped design.

The second is sending too many design references without explaining which details matter. Choose one photo, name the colours or piping feature you want the baker to interpret, and send the exact inscription. White Cakes hand-pipes buttercream; a reference is guidance, not a promise of a perfect copy or edible printing from a file.

The third is treating delivery as a vague instruction. Send a full address, landmark, receiver's mobile number, and the correct gate or venue entrance. Pick someone who can accept the cake during the agreed window and move it out of direct sun. If the event venue has entry rules, arrange those before the driver arrives.

Finally, do not assume a cake size from the number of invitations. Tell the bakery how many people will eat cake and whether it will be served after a meal; use the published size guide to discuss portions. The order is confirmed only after availability, design, price, and logistics are agreed and the deposit is received. Keep these practical notes in the Sinulog and central ordering guides; avoid claiming particular traffic closures or event access rules unless they have been verified for that year's route.
