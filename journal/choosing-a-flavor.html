Choosing a flavor

Chocolate, vanilla, ube, mango, and red velvet. The outside can stay ivory. The choice is what the cake tastes like when it is cut.

The cakes on this site are finished in ivory buttercream. Marble, roses, and shells sit on that cream. Flavor is the inside: the crumb and the filling. We bake five. Chocolate, vanilla, ube, mango, and red velvet. You name one when you order. If the table is split, say so in the notes and we will tell you whether a second flavor is possible for that size. A 4-inch bento is one flavor. A 6-inch cake can sometimes be divided, and we would rather confirm that than imply it.

Chocolate

Choose chocolate when the cake has to please the widest table. Birthdays with children and adults, office days, anyone who asked for “a real cake” and meant cocoa. It is the safest flavor if you do not know every guest, and it still tastes like chocolate after a ride across Cebu, which matters more than people expect. The ivory outside does not announce it. The slice does.

Vanilla

Choose vanilla when the design should lead and the flavor should stay quiet. It is the right cake for a bouquet of roses, for a first birthday where the color is the point, and for anyone who finds chocolate heavy in the afternoon heat. Vanilla is not a blank. It is what you want when the inscription and the flowers are doing the talking, and when you expect people to eat more than a polite bite.

Ube

Ube is the flavor people in Cebu already know from halo-halo, jam, and home baking. Order it when the celebration is a family table and someone at that table would be disappointed by a cake that could have been served anywhere. The crumb is purple. We do not hide that under a story. It is not a novelty topping on a vanilla cake. If half the guests have never tasted ube, chocolate or vanilla will be kinder. If they grew up with it, ube is the one they will remember.

Mango

Mango is for a table that wants fruit instead of cocoa. It is a Cebu taste in the plain sense: the fruit is local, people already like it, and it does not need explaining. It is brightest when you are serving people who would pick mango over chocolate on an ordinary day. The cream is a little softer in heat than a chocolate cake, so plan the pickup close to the meal, or read the note on keeping buttercream cool. Do not order mango if the cake will sit on a sunny buffet for hours. It will still be cake. It will not be at its best.

Red velvet

Red velvet is the cake someone asks for by name. Mild cocoa, a red crumb, a celebration color that only shows when you cut it. Choose it when a guest has requested “the red one,” for an anniversary that wants a classic, or when chocolate feels too dark and vanilla feels too plain. It is not a different shape. It is the same ivory finish, with a slice that explains itself.

If you are still unsure, use this order. Mixed ages and no strong opinion: chocolate. A quiet design, or a hot afternoon: vanilla. A family that asks for ube: ube. Fruit, and a short trip from us to the table: mango. A named request: red velvet. Write the choice in the order. If you want the filling different from the crumb, say that in the notes and we will tell you whether we can do it for your date.

Name the flavor in the order. We confirm within a day.
